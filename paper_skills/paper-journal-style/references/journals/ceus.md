# CEUS Guidance

Use this file for Computers, Environment and Urban Systems style checks when the user explicitly targets CEUS.

## Positioning

Favor an applied urban-computing framing:

- Make the urban problem and practical significance explicit
- Keep the method description concrete but concise
- Tie findings to planning, environmental, mobility, or urban-management relevance when the draft supports that claim

## Abstract Focus

A strong CEUS-facing abstract usually makes these points clear:

- Urban problem or decision context
- Data source or study setting
- Method family or modeling idea
- Main empirical finding
- Practical or scientific contribution

Avoid spending too much abstract space on generic motivation that does not narrow to the specific urban problem.

## Highlights Focus

Prefer factual highlights that cover different angles:

- Problem or task definition
- Data or study scale
- Method novelty
- Main empirical gain or insight
- Urban implication when supported

Do not repeat the same contribution across multiple bullets with different wording.

## Consistency Checks

For CEUS-style submissions, pay extra attention to:

- Urban terminology staying stable across title, abstract, and conclusion
- Geographic units, sample sizes, and dataset coverage matching everywhere
- Claimed policy or planning implications remaining proportional to the evidence
- Method novelty claims not drifting beyond what the paper actually introduces

## Safe Output Pattern

If exact author-guide limits are not available in local references:

- Give a conservative compliance review
- Flag any likely overlength or formatting risk as `needs author verification`
- Avoid asserting exact submission limits from memory
