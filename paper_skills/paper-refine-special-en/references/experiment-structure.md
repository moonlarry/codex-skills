# Experiment Structure

Apply this reference whenever the text is about experimental results, ablations, sensitivity studies, efficiency analysis, or case studies.

## Mandatory Paragraph Pattern

Every experiment paragraph must follow this order:

1. Conclusion sentence
2. Figure or table evidence
3. Interpretation

This is mandatory, not optional.

## Practical Rules

- Start each paragraph by stating the main finding in one sentence.
- Immediately support that finding with figure numbers, table numbers, explicit values, or comparison targets.
- End by explaining what the evidence means, why the trend appears, or what boundary/tradeoff it reveals.

## Multi-Paragraph Experiment Subsection

For a subsection with two or more paragraphs, impose progression across paragraphs as well:

- Paragraph 1: overall finding or headline result
- Paragraph 2: key component or ablation evidence
- Paragraph 3: interpretation, robustness, tradeoff, or boundary case

If the original text reports raw numbers before making a claim, reorder it.

If the evidence is too weak for the stated conclusion, reduce the strength of the claim during the reviewer-style pass.
