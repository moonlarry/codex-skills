# Global Logic Check

Use this reference whenever the user gives more than a single paragraph, and still apply it in lighter form for single-paragraph inputs.

## What To Check

- Paragraph sequencing inside a subsection
- Section sequencing inside a chapter or paper
- Whether each paragraph has a clear role
- Whether adjacent paragraphs overlap too much or leave a gap
- Whether a section heading matches what the section actually proves
- Whether earlier claims are validated by later experiments
- Whether terminology changes across sections without explanation

## Typical Failure Modes

- Claim paragraph appears before setup or evidence
- Two adjacent paragraphs make nearly the same point
- A paragraph reports numbers without first stating the conclusion
- The section ends without interpreting why the results matter
- The paper claims a contribution in the introduction that is never convincingly validated later

## Rewrite Strategy

- Merge paragraphs with the same function
- Split overloaded paragraphs that mix claim, evidence, and interpretation without order
- Add explicit transitions when the reader would otherwise have to infer a missing link
- Downgrade claims when evidence is not strong enough

## Whole-Section And Whole-Paper Rule

When polishing a full section or paper, optimize for argument flow, not local sentence beauty alone. If local wording is good but global logic is weak, fix the structure first.
